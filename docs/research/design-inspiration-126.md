# ContextShield visual research: 126 live-site references

Date: 3 September 2026

## Method

The breadth pass reviewed current homepage captures and metadata across six pages of the A1 landing-page gallery, then removed sponsored templates and duplicate listings. This produced 126 live product, technology, security, editorial and experimental references. A second pass used the dedicated AI and security collections to identify category conventions. A focused live-page pass then inspected XBOW, Tracebit, LangChain and SISA for information hierarchy, proof placement and trust language.

This is an inspiration ledger, not a claim that ContextShield copied any design. The reusable lessons were:

- Lead with one concrete, falsifiable promise.
- Demonstrate the product within the first screen.
- Use motion to explain a state change, not as decoration.
- Put benchmarks and their scope beside each other.
- Let typography and one visual motif do most of the brand work.
- Treat privacy architecture and honest limitations as proof, not footer material.
- Avoid the generic purple glow, chat mockup and unearned logo wall common to AI sites.

## What entered the ContextShield design

The redesign uses a single electric-mint signal colour on an obsidian canvas, oversized editorial type, a real animated privacy transformation in the hero, a live system beacon, benchmark tiles mapped to the SIH weights, and an interactive local-versus-server proof. The recurring visual motif is the privacy aperture: raw data moves toward a strict circular gate and emerges as a small typed packet. No logos, customer claims or decorative AI imagery were fabricated.
